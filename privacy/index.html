Privacy

PocketDeck has no account, no analytics, no ads and no crash reporting. There is no server of ours: the app talks to the services below directly from your phone.

Stays on your phone

  • Your collection (per account), saved decks and settings.
  • Your API keys for the AI services, in the app's private storage. They are not included in any Android backup, and the app does not back up anything (Android backup is switched off). To move to a new phone, use Export and Import in the app.
  • While a scan runs, the app reads the game's screen on your phone to recognise cards. Nothing from the screen is stored or sent; only the recognised card counts are kept.

Downloaded from other services

Each of these sees your phone's IP address and the request itself:

  • Limitless TCG (play.limitlesstcg.com): the recent tournament decklists.
  • TCGdex (assets.tcgdex.net) and GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com): card images. Images are kept on the phone after the first download.
  • OpenRouter (openrouter.ai): the public model list, only when you open the model picker.
  • Anthropic (api.anthropic.com): the list of Claude models your key can use, only when you open that picker.
  • OpenAI (api.openai.com): the list of models your key can use, only when you open that picker.
  • Your own server, if you set one up (Ollama, LM Studio, ...): the list of its models, only when you open that picker.

Sent only if you use the AI feature

If you enter an API key and ask the AI for a deck, the app sends your card list, the tournament decks and your optional wish text, together with your key, to the service you chose (Anthropic, OpenAI or OpenRouter) or to the server you set up yourself. That service's own privacy policy applies from then on. OpenRouter's free models, and the providers behind them, may log what you send. Do not use the AI feature if you do not want your card list leaving the phone.

A server of your own is only reached at the address you type. If that address starts with http:// (no encryption), the app uses it only after you switch on "Allow plain http" in Settings, and anyone on that network could then read what is sent, including a key. An answer that is not a valid deck is sent back for correction (up to 3 tries), and if a model fails the next model or service on your list is asked as well, so one request can send the card list more than once. Paid services charge for every token.

Auto-scroll

Auto-scroll is an optional feature that swipes the game's card list for you while a scan runs, so you do not have to scroll by hand. It uses Android's Accessibility service only for that, reads nothing on the screen and collects nothing. It is off until you agree, and you can switch it off in the system's Accessibility settings at any time. Automating input in a game may go against that game's terms; you use it at your own risk.
